# The Textured Crop (French Crop): A Complete Guide

The textured crop is one of the few modern cuts that's genuinely easy to live with. It's short, it's brought forward, it needs about thirty seconds of styling, and it works in an office as well as it does on a weekend.

It also solves a few problems. If your hair grows forward at the front, has a cowlick you're tired of fighting, or your hairline sits higher than it used to, a crop works with all of that instead of against it.

It isn't for everyone, though, and the details matter. How long the fringe is, whether it's cut blunt or textured, and what goes on the sides all change the result. Here's what to know before you ask for one.

## What a Textured Crop Is

A crop is a short haircut where the hair on top is cut to a fairly even length and pushed **forward**, ending in a short fringe across the forehead. The sides are shorter, usually faded or tapered.

The **French crop** is the classic version: a neat, slightly longer top with a straight, defined fringe. The **textured crop** is the modern version: the top is cut with a lot of texture, using point-cutting or texturizing shears, so it looks broken-up and messy rather than smooth.

In practice the two names are used interchangeably. The difference people actually notice is in the fringe.

## Fringe Length: The Most Important Decision

The fringe is the line the eye goes to first, so its length changes the whole look.

| Fringe length | Where it sits | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Very short (under 1 cm) | Well above the brow, at the top of the forehead | Sharp, military-adjacent, low-maintenance |
| Short (1–2 cm) | Upper forehead | The standard crop; clean and versatile |
| Mid (2–4 cm) | Mid-forehead | Softer, more fashion-forward, hides more forehead |
| Long (to the brow) | Near or touching the eyebrows | Heavier, more youthful; needs more upkeep |

A practical guide: the higher or more uneven your hairline, the longer the fringe should be. If your hairline is solid and even, a shorter fringe looks crisp.

## Blunt Fringe vs Textured Fringe

**Blunt fringe:** cut in a straight, solid line across the forehead. It looks graphic, deliberate and a bit retro. It suits straight hair best, because the line stays clean. On wavy or curly hair, a blunt fringe often bends and kinks and ends up looking uneven.

**Textured fringe:** point-cut so the edge is broken up and uneven by design. It's more forgiving, it grows out more gracefully, and it suits wavy and thick hair much better. If you're not sure which one you want, this is the safer choice.

A good barber will often recommend one over the other based on how your hair falls at the front, and they'll be right more often than a reference photo of someone else will.

## Which Fade to Pair It With

The crop's top is short, so the sides need to be shorter to give it shape. Almost all crops come with a fade or taper.

- **High skin fade:** the most common pairing and the sharpest. Tight sides make the top look denser and give the cut a clear outline.
- **Mid fade:** a bit softer, suits most head shapes, and grows out more slowly. A good default if you're not sure.
- **Low taper:** the most conservative choice. It keeps the sides fuller and makes the crop look more classic than modern.

If you're choosing between those, our guide to [skin fade vs taper fade](/skin-fade-vs-taper-fade) explains how the finish changes the look and the maintenance.

## Hair Types It Suits (And Doesn't)

**Excellent fit:**

- **Thick, straight hair.** The ideal texture: it holds the shape, takes texture well and covers the scalp.
- **Wavy hair.** The waves add texture you'd otherwise have to create with product. A textured crop on wavy hair often needs barely any styling.
- **Forward-growing hair and front cowlicks.** If your hair naturally falls forward or flips up at the front, a crop goes with that direction instead of fighting it.
- **A higher or receding hairline.** Bringing the hair forward with a fringe covers the temples and the front of the hairline. This is one of the reasons the crop is so often recommended on our [receding hairline guide](/best-haircuts-receding-hairline).

**Weaker fit:**

- **Very fine or thin hair on top.** Short, textured hair shows scalp easily. A crop can still work, but expect a shorter, neater version rather than a big, choppy one.
- **Tight curls and coily hair.** A textured crop in the usual sense doesn't really apply. A short curly top with a fade gets a similar silhouette and is the better request.
- **A crown cowlick that grows backward.** The crop needs hair to fall forward, and a strong crown swirl can fight that. It's workable, but the top usually needs to stay a bit longer to weigh it down.

Not sure which category you're in? Our [men's hair types guide](/mens-hair-types-guide) is a good place to start.

## Styling: Matte Product, Light Hand

The textured crop looks best with a matte, dry finish. Shine makes it look stiff.

1. **Start with dry or towel-dried hair.** Wet hair and clay don't mix well.
2. **Use a small amount**, about the size of a fingertip, of matte clay or paste. Rub it between your palms until it's warm and almost invisible.
3. **Work it in from back to front**, pushing the hair forward with your fingertips.
4. **Pinch and twist small sections** on top to separate them and build texture.
5. **Settle the fringe last.** Push it forward and tousle it slightly so it doesn't sit in one solid sheet.

On straight hair that lies flat, a sea salt spray on damp hair before blow-drying gives the clay more to grip. On wavy hair you can often skip the clay entirely.

## How It Grows Out

The top of a crop is short, so it starts to lose its shape at around four weeks, when the fringe starts heading toward your eyebrows and the texture softens. The faded sides need attention sooner: about two to three weeks for a high skin fade, three to four for a taper.

Many people with crops book a cut every three weeks and call it done.

## How to Ask for It

Cover the sides, the top and the fringe:

- "**Textured crop**, high skin fade, about an inch and a half on top, short textured fringe."
- "**French crop**, mid taper, blunt fringe at the upper forehead."
- "**Crop with a longer fringe** to cover my hairline, texture it, low fade."

Bring a photo showing the front so the fringe length is visible.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is a textured crop good for a receding hairline?**
It's one of the best options. The forward fringe covers the temples and the front of the hairline. Keep the fringe at mid length rather than very short.

**What's the difference between a French crop and a Caesar cut?**
They're closely related. A Caesar is usually shorter and more uniform, with a straight, short fringe. The French crop is a little longer and is often textured.

**Can I wear a textured crop with a beard?**
Yes, and it's a very popular combination. The short top and faded sides make a full beard look more balanced. Just keep the beard trimmed so its length matches the short, tidy feel of the top.

**Do I need product?**
For most hair types, a small amount of matte product makes a big difference. Wavy hair can sometimes go without.

**How long does my hair need to be to get a crop?**
About an inch and a half to two inches on top is enough to cut a proper textured crop.
