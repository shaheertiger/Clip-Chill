# How to Grow Out Your Hair Without the Awkward Stage

Every man who has grown his hair out remembers the moment he nearly quit. It usually arrives around month two or three: the sides are puffing out over the ears, the back is curling over the collar, the top hasn't caught up, and nothing you do in the morning makes it look intentional.

Here's the honest version: you can't skip the awkward stage entirely. Hair grows roughly half an inch a month, and getting from a fade to anything you can tuck behind your ears takes the better part of a year. What you can do is make every stage look deliberate instead of neglected — and that's mostly about shape, not length.

The men who grow their hair out successfully are almost never the ones who avoided the barber for eight months. They're the ones who kept going, and told the barber exactly what they were doing.

## Why You Still Need Trims

The most common mistake is assuming that any cut works against you. It doesn't — as long as the barber is cutting for shape, not length.

Hair on the sides and back grows at roughly the same rate as hair on top, but it has a much shorter distance to travel before it looks messy. Left alone, the sides bulk out first and the back outgrows the top, which gives you the mushroom or mullet-by-accident shape that makes people give up.

A grow-out trim fixes that without costing you progress:

- **Tidying the neckline** so the back reads as clean rather than shaggy.
- **Cleaning around the ears** so the sides don't look overgrown.
- **Keeping the back and sides in proportion to the top**, so the top can catch up and become the longest part of the cut.
- **Removing split or damaged ends** on the top only when they genuinely need it.

Expect to see a barber every six to eight weeks during a grow-out, rather than the three to four weeks you'd keep for a fade.

## Month by Month: What to Expect

Starting from a typical short cut — a fade or taper with two to three inches on top:

| Stage | What it looks like | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Months 1–2** | Fade has grown in; sides feel full; top is unchanged in style | Let the sides grow. One tidy-up of the neckline and ears. |
| **Months 3–4** | Sides push out over the ears; back touches the collar | The hardest stretch. Trim the back and ears; leave the top alone. |
| **Months 5–6** | Top can be pushed back or to the side; sides can be tucked or slicked | Starts to look like a style. Light shaping and weight removal. |
| **Months 7–9** | Medium length; top reaches the eyebrows or past | Decide on the final shape: layered, one length, or swept back. |
| **Months 10–12+** | Chin length or longer, depending on texture | Trims every two to three months, mostly for ends. |

These timings assume straight or slightly wavy hair. Curly and coily hair takes longer to *look* long because of shrinkage, which is covered below.

## What to Tell Your Barber

The single most useful sentence you can say is: **"I'm growing it out — I want to keep the length on top and just clean up the shape."**

Then be specific:

- **How long you've been growing it** and roughly where you want to end up. A photo of the end goal helps more than anything.
- **No clippers on the sides**, unless you specifically want a tapered neckline. Scissor work keeps the length you've earned.
- **Which part bothers you most.** If it's the puff over the ears, say so. If it's the back curling out, say that.
- **"Take as little as possible off the top."** Most barbers will happily dust the ends rather than cut length.

If you're not sure how to phrase any of this, our guide on [how to ask for a haircut](/how-to-ask-for-a-haircut) covers the vocabulary.

A good barber will also tell you when the back needs to come up a bit more than you'd like. That's usually the right call — a slightly shorter back is what lets the top look longer.

## Products for the In-Between Phase

Product is what turns months three to five from bad to manageable.

- **Sea salt spray** — adds texture and grip to hair that's too long to sit flat and too short to fall properly. Works well for straight and wavy hair.
- **Matte paste or clay** — light hold for pushing the top back or to the side without shine. Use a small amount; too much makes medium hair look greasy.
- **Leave-in conditioner or curl cream** — for curly and coily hair, this matters more than hold. Defined curls look intentional at almost any length.
- **A light cream or styling balm** — for the later stages when you want some control but movement too.

Blow-drying in the direction you want the hair to sit, even for a minute, often does more than any product. Our [at-home styling guide](/how-to-style-mens-hair-at-home) goes into the technique.

## Hats, Headbands and Other Coping Strategies

Let's be honest about these.

**Hats** are fine occasionally. Wearing one every day hides the problem without solving it, and tight caps can flatten hair and leave it oily. If you're wearing a hat to avoid seeing your hair, that's usually a sign you need a shaping trim, not more time under the hat.

**Headbands and elastic hair bands** work for some men in months four to seven, particularly for workouts. They're a personal style choice — some men pull them off, some don't.

**Pushing it back with product** is the most universally flattering option once you have three to four inches on top. It hides uneven lengths at the front better than anything else.

**Tucking behind the ears** becomes possible around month five or six for straight hair, and immediately makes the sides look intentional.

## Patience by Hair Type

How long the awkward stage lasts depends heavily on texture.

- **Straight hair** shows length fastest but also shows unevenness most. The sides tend to stick straight out around months three and four. Regular shaping matters most here.
- **Wavy hair** often has the easiest grow-out, because waves hide uneven lengths. Months three to five can actually look good with a little sea salt spray.
- **Curly hair** can shrink to half its actual length or less, so it looks shorter for longer. The upside is that curls grow out into a rounded shape that reads as a style. Our [curly hair guide](/curly-hair-mens-haircut-guide) has more.
- **Coily hair** grows outward before it grows down. Expect the shape to be more about volume than length for the first several months, and keep it moisturized.
- **Fine or thinning hair** can look thinner as it gets longer. Be realistic about the end goal, and ask your barber whether a medium length will suit you better than a long one.

## When to Call It Off

Sometimes the honest answer is that long hair isn't working for you. Signs it may be time to reconsider:

- Thinning or recession is becoming more visible as length increases.
- Your hair texture makes it impossible to manage without an hour of work.
- You've passed six months and still don't like any version of it.

There's no shame in going back to a fade. A medium-length cut — scissor-cut sides and two to four inches on top — is also a perfectly good place to stop.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long does it take to grow out men's hair?**
Roughly six months to reach a medium, pushed-back length from a short fade, and about a year to reach chin length. Curly and coily hair takes longer to look long because of shrinkage.

**Do trims make your hair grow faster?**
No. Trims don't change the growth rate. What they do is keep the shape balanced and remove damaged ends, so the length you have looks better.

**Should I grow out the sides or the top first?**
The top needs to stay longest, so the sides and back get trimmed lightly while the top is left alone. That way the top catches up and the proportions stay right.

**How often should I see a barber while growing my hair out?**
Every six to eight weeks for most men. Later in the grow-out, every two to three months is usually enough.

**Can I grow out a skin fade?**
Yes. Expect the sides to look fuzzy for the first few weeks; one light tidy-up around week four or five helps the transition.
