# Going Grey: Haircuts and Beard Care for Grey and Salt-and-Pepper Hair

Grey hair has a reputation problem it doesn't deserve. Plenty of men worry the first silver strands will make them look older or tired. What usually makes grey look tired isn't the colour. It's grey hair that's a bit too long, a bit yellow, and a bit unkempt.

Kept sharp, grey reads as confident and put-together. Left to its own devices, it reads as neglect. The difference is mostly maintenance, and most of that maintenance is simple.

Here's what changes when hair goes grey, and what to do about it from a barber's point of view.

## Why Grey Hair Feels Different

Grey or white hair is hair that's growing with little or no pigment. That's the colour change. But most men notice a texture change too, and it's real.

- **Coarser and wirier.** Many grey strands feel thicker and stiffer than the hair around them, and they often stick up or out at a different angle.
- **Drier.** The scalp's oil production tends to drop with age, so grey hair is often drier and frizzier.
- **Less predictable.** Grey strands can have a slightly different wave or direction to the pigmented hair next to them, which is why salt-and-pepper hair can look wiry even on a fresh cut.

It varies from person to person, and some men's grey hair stays soft. But if your hair has started behaving differently as it greys, that's why, and it changes how it should be cut and cared for.

**Practical fixes:**

- Use a **conditioner** every time you shampoo. It's the single biggest improvement for wiry grey hair.
- Shampoo **two or three times a week**, not daily, to avoid stripping what little oil there is.
- A light **leave-in cream** helps tame the strands that stick out.

## Yellowing and Purple Shampoo

Grey and white hair has no pigment to mask anything, so it picks up tints easily. The usual causes of yellowing:

- Sun exposure
- Smoke, including second-hand and cooking smoke
- Hard water minerals
- Product buildup, especially from heavy or tinted products
- Heat from hot tools

**Purple shampoo** is the standard fix. Purple sits opposite yellow on the colour wheel, so a small amount of violet pigment cancels out the yellow and leaves grey looking cleaner and brighter.

How to use it:

1. Once a week, swap your normal shampoo for purple shampoo.
2. Lather and leave it for **one to three minutes**, then rinse.
3. Follow with conditioner. Purple shampoos can be drying.

Don't overdo it. Using it every day, or leaving it on too long, can give light grey or white hair a faint lilac tint. If that happens, wash a couple of times with regular shampoo and it fades.

## Cuts That Flatter Grey

Grey hair looks best when it looks intentional. A few principles:

**Shorter on the sides.** Grey tends to show first at the temples and sides. Keeping them tight with a taper or fade makes that grey look neat and controlled rather than fluffy. Short sides also contrast nicely against a slightly longer top.

**A taper over a skin fade, usually.** A skin fade looks sharp on anyone, but the contrast between grey hair and bare skin is lower than with dark hair, so the fade effect is subtler. A taper often looks more polished on salt-and-pepper. Our [skin fade vs taper fade](/skin-fade-vs-taper-fade) guide covers the difference.

**Classic shapes.** The side part, the short textured crop, the ivy league, and the short brush-back all suit grey well. They're tidy, they look good at any age, and they make the most of the silver.

**Texture to tame wiriness.** Point-cutting the top helps grey strands sit together instead of springing out individually.

**Watch the length.** Long, untidy grey hair is the fastest way to look older. Grey hair needs a little more frequent cutting than darker hair to stay crisp, because frizz and stray strands show more.

## Keep It Sharp

This is where grey either looks distinguished or doesn't. The details that matter:

- **Neckline and around the ears.** Grey edges that grow fuzzy look untidy quickly. Keep them clean.
- **Eyebrows.** Brows often get longer and wirier around the same time hair goes grey. Face threading ($15) tidies the strays cleanly without an over-shaped look.
- **Ear and nose hair.** Not glamorous, but worth mentioning. Stray grey hairs here are very noticeable.
- **Regular cuts.** Every three to four weeks keeps a short grey cut looking deliberate.

## Grey Beard Patterns

Beards rarely go grey evenly. Common patterns:

- **Chin first.** A grey patch at the chin, or two stripes running down from the corners of the mouth, is extremely common.
- **Sideburns and cheeks.** Grey often appears where the beard meets the hair.
- **Mixed throughout.** Some men get an even salt-and-pepper across the whole beard.

None of these is a problem. Uneven grey actually adds character and depth to a beard, as long as the shape is clean.

**Beard care for grey:**

- **Keep it shorter and well-defined.** Grey beard hair is often coarser and wirier than the rest, so length gets unruly faster. A clean cheek line and neckline make the grey look deliberate.
- **Use beard oil or balm.** It softens coarse grey hair and reduces the scratchy feel.
- **Purple shampoo works on beards too**, used occasionally, if your beard picks up yellow from smoke or coffee.
- **Trim regularly.** Every two to three weeks is a good rhythm.

## Dye vs Embrace: The Honest Take

There's no right answer. Some men want to hold onto their natural colour for a while longer, and that's a fair choice. But here's what's worth knowing before you decide.

**Full-coverage dye often looks obvious on men.** Hair that's a single flat, dark colour with no variation can look artificial, especially against a lined face or a grey beard. Short men's cuts also show root regrowth fast. Within a couple of weeks, a grey line appears at the scalp and around the hairline.

**Blending is more convincing than covering.** Products designed to blend grey rather than eliminate it leave some silver showing and fade gradually, so the result looks more natural and the regrowth is softer.

**A matching beard matters.** Dyed hair with a grey beard, or the reverse, is the combination that looks most obviously coloured.

**Patch test anything.** Hair dyes can cause allergic reactions. Follow the product's patch test instructions every time, and stop if your scalp reacts.

**Embracing it is easier than you think.** Many men who stop colouring find the grey looks better than they expected once it's cut short and kept tidy. Going shorter also speeds up the transition, because the dyed ends are cut away sooner.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does plucking a grey hair make more grow back?**
No. Plucking doesn't affect the hairs around it. It can damage the follicle over time, though, so it's better to trim a stray grey than pull it.

**Why are my grey hairs curlier than the rest?**
Grey strands can grow with a slightly different texture and shape. It's common and harmless. A shorter cut and a light cream keep it under control.

**Can stress make you go grey?**
Stress is often blamed, and research suggests it may play a role, but genetics is the main factor in when most men go grey. If you notice sudden, unusual greying along with other changes in your health, mention it to your family doctor.

**Should I change my haircut when I go grey?**
Not necessarily, but a little shorter and a little tidier tends to suit grey better. Ask your barber for an honest opinion.

**Is purple shampoo safe for every hair colour?**
It's designed for grey, white, and blond hair. On dark hair it won't do much.
