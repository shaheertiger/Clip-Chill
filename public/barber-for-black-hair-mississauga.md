## Why Coily Hair Needs a Different Approach

Afro-textured hair, including the tightly coiled 4c pattern, behaves differently from straight or wavy hair in almost every way that matters to a barber.

**It's dense and it stands up.** Coily hair grows out from the scalp rather than lying flat, so a #2 guard on 4c hair looks and reads nothing like a #2 on straight hair.

**It shrinks.** Coily hair can look much shorter than its actual length. That's why experienced barbers often cut it dry, and why many will pick out the top with an Afro pick before cutting, so the length is even when it settles back down.

**Lines show up.** On dark, dense hair against skin, every transition is visible. A fade that would look fine on lighter, finer hair can show obvious shelves on coily hair if the blending isn't done carefully.

**The skin underneath matters.** Curly and coily facial hair is the most prone to razor bumps, which changes how the line-up and beard work should be done. More on that below.

## The Cuts and Details Worth Asking About

### Fades on Coily Hair

A good fade on coily hair is smooth from the lowest guard into the length on top, with no visible steps. Because the hair is dense, blending usually takes more passes with more guard sizes and half-guards, not fewer. If you want the bottom to go all the way to skin, that's a skin fade; if you want it to stay a little darker, that's a taper. Our [skin fade vs taper fade](/skin-fade-vs-taper-fade) guide covers the difference.

### Line-Ups and Shape-Ups

The line-up, or shape-up, is the crisp edge around the forehead, temples and behind the ears. The single most common complaint people have with an inexperienced barber is the hairline creeping back a little more every visit. A barber who knows what they're doing follows your natural hairline and cleans it up rather than cutting a new one higher each time.

Tell the barber whether you want it natural and soft or sharp and defined.

### Waves

If you're growing waves, the barber's job is mostly to stay out of the way. That means a low, even cut on top, going with the grain of your brush pattern, not against it. If you're in the middle of "wolfing" (growing it out to deepen the pattern), say so, so they only tidy the edges.

### Twists and Sponge Texture

Sponge twists and coils need enough length on top to grab with a sponge. Ask for the sides faded and the top left long enough, and say so before the clippers come out.

## Beard Bumps and Why Clipper Over Razor Matters

Pseudofolliculitis barbae is the medical name for razor bumps. It happens when a curly hair is cut very short, then curls back and grows into the skin instead of out of it. It's especially common on coily facial hair, and it's often worse on the neck and along the line-up.

- **Cutting at or below skin level triggers it.** Shaving against the grain, or a zero-gap trimmer pressed hard into the skin, leaves sharp tips right at the surface.
- **Leaving a tiny bit of length helps.** A clipper or trimmer that cuts just above the skin is often a better choice than a razor finish if you're prone to bumps.
- **The line-up edge is the risky spot.** A sharp beard line or neck line done with a razor looks great on day one, but if you're prone to bumps, a trimmer-finished line may look better a week later.

A good barber will ask whether you get bumps before reaching for a blade. If they don't ask, tell them. You can read more in our guide to [razor bumps and ingrown hairs](/razor-bumps-ingrown-hairs-men).

## Searching "Black Barber Shop Mississauga"? How to Vet a Barber

**Look for photos of your hair type.** Instagram and Google photos are the portfolio. Plenty of shops post nothing but straight-hair fades. You want to see clean fades, line-ups and beard work on coily hair specifically, ideally more than one example.

**Read reviews for specifics.** A review that says "great cut" tells you very little. One that mentions a fade on 4c hair, a line-up that stayed natural, or a barber's name tells you a lot.

**Ask a couple of questions.** A short call or a minute at the chair before you start is fair:

- How would you blend a fade on hair like mine?
- Do you finish line-ups with a razor or a trimmer?
- How do you handle someone who gets bumps?

A barber who's comfortable with coily hair will answer these easily and specifically. Vague answers are useful information too.

## What to Bring and What to Say

- **A photo** of the cut you want, on hair like yours. A photo of a fade on straight hair won't translate.
- **Your usual guard numbers**, if you know them, and how long since your last cut.
- **Your hairline preference:** natural or sharp, and whether it's been pushed back before.
- **Your skin history:** bumps, sensitivity, or anything that's reacted badly before.
- **What you're growing:** waves, twists, a beard, so the barber protects it.

**Why does my hairline keep getting pushed back?**
