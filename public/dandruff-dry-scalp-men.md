# Dandruff or Dry Scalp? How to Tell and What to Do

Barbers see a lot of scalps. And one of the most common things we notice in the chair — usually before the client has mentioned it — is flaking. Sometimes it's mild, sometimes it's been going on for months, and very often the person has been treating the wrong problem.

Dandruff and dry scalp look similar at a glance but have different causes and need different fixes. Using an anti-dandruff shampoo on a dry scalp can make it drier. Piling moisturizing products onto dandruff can make it worse. Working out which one you have is the first step.

The usual note: we're barbers, not doctors. This is general, well-established guidance. If your scalp is painful, inflamed, bleeding or not improving, see a family doctor or dermatologist.

## How to Tell the Difference

| | **Dandruff** | **Dry scalp** |
|---|---|---|
| **Flake size** | Larger, often clumped | Small, fine, powdery |
| **Flake colour/feel** | Yellowish or white, slightly oily | White, dry |
| **Scalp feel** | Often oily, sometimes red | Tight, dry, itchy |
| **Other dry skin?** | Not necessarily | Often — dry hands, face, shins |
| **Where else?** | Eyebrows, sides of nose, beard, behind ears | Usually just the scalp |
| **Seasonal pattern** | Can flare in winter, but present year-round | Much worse in winter |

The quick test: if your scalp is oily and your flakes are bigger and slightly greasy, it's more likely dandruff. If your scalp feels tight and the flakes are fine and dry — and the rest of your skin is dry too — it's more likely dry scalp.

Many people have a bit of both, particularly in winter.

## What Causes Dandruff

Dandruff is very common and isn't a sign of poor hygiene. It's generally linked to:

- **A yeast called Malassezia** that lives on most people's scalps. In some people, the scalp reacts to it with faster skin turnover, which produces flakes.
- **Oil production.** The yeast feeds on scalp oil, which is why dandruff tends to show up on oilier scalps.
- **Seborrheic dermatitis**, a more pronounced form, which can also cause redness and flaking on the face, eyebrows and beard.
- **Stress, poor sleep and cold weather**, which commonly make flare-ups worse.

## What Causes Dry Scalp

Dry scalp is simpler: the skin is short on moisture. Common causes:

- **Cold, dry air** and low humidity.
- **Indoor heating**, which dries the air further.
- **Hot showers**, which strip natural oil.
- **Washing too often** with harsh shampoo.
- **Some styling products and fragrances**, which can irritate sensitive skin.

## Canadian Winters Make Both Worse

In Mississauga, flaking clients pick up noticeably from November onward. Cold air outside, forced-air heating inside, long hot showers and toques pulled on and off all day — it's close to the worst possible combination for a scalp.

Practical winter adjustments:

- **Turn the shower temperature down.** Warm, not hot.
- **Run a humidifier** in the bedroom if your home is very dry.
- **Wash hats and toques** regularly, since they hold oil and flakes against the scalp.
- **Use a conditioner every time you shampoo**, keeping it mostly on the lengths if your scalp is oily.

## Shampoo Ingredients That Help

For dandruff, look for an over-the-counter anti-dandruff shampoo containing one of these common active ingredients:

- **Zinc pyrithione** — a common, gentle starting point.
- **Ketoconazole** — an antifungal that targets the yeast.
- **Selenium sulfide** — slows skin turnover and targets yeast.
- **Salicylic acid** — loosens and lifts flakes; useful for thicker buildup.
- **Coal tar** — slows skin cell turnover; strong-smelling, and not for everyone.

For dry scalp, the approach is different: a **gentle, moisturizing shampoo**, less frequent washing and a conditioner. Medicated shampoos may be too drying if dryness is the only problem.

If you're unsure which you have, a pharmacist can help you pick, and a doctor can confirm.

## How to Use Medicated Shampoo Properly

Most people who say anti-dandruff shampoo "didn't work" used it like regular shampoo. A few rules make a real difference:

1. **Give it contact time.** Lather it into the scalp — not just the hair — and leave it for the time on the label, often around five minutes, before rinsing.
2. **Massage, don't scratch.** Use your fingertips, not your nails.
3. **Use it consistently.** Two or three times a week, alternating with a regular gentle shampoo on other days.
4. **Rotate if it stops working.** Some people find a product becomes less effective over time; switching between two different active ingredients can help.
5. **Give it a few weeks.** Improvement is gradual. Once things settle, many people can drop to once a week for maintenance.
6. **Follow the label.** Some ingredients can affect colour-treated or light hair, and some aren't meant for daily use.

## Wash Frequency and Product Buildup

There isn't a single right number of washes per week. It depends on your scalp:

- **Oily scalp with dandruff** often does better with more frequent washing, because oil feeds the problem.
- **Dry scalp** usually does better with less frequent washing and cooler water.

**Product buildup** is a third cause that's easy to miss. Heavy waxes, pomades and dry shampoo can build up on the scalp, especially with short hair, and come off as white flakes that look like dandruff. If you use a lot of product, a clarifying wash once a week often fixes the "flakes" on its own.

## Why Your Barber Notices First

You can't see the top or back of your own head clearly. Your barber spends twenty minutes looking at it from every angle, under good light, often with your hair parted down to the scalp. That's why barbers are often the first to spot flaking, redness, or patches that look different from the rest of the scalp.

A good barber will mention it discreetly. A **Hair Wash & Styling** at the shop is a good time to ask what they're seeing, and our barbers can tell you what they're seeing — but diagnosing it is a job for a doctor.

## When to See a Doctor

See a family doctor or dermatologist if:

- Over-the-counter shampoos haven't helped after several weeks of proper use.
- Your scalp is red, swollen, painful, oozing or bleeding.
- You have thick, silvery or crusted patches — this can be psoriasis or another condition.
- Flaking is spreading to your face, ears, chest or beard in a way that's getting worse.
- You're losing hair along with the flaking.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is dandruff caused by not washing enough?**
Not exactly. Dandruff isn't a hygiene problem, but infrequent washing can let oil and flakes build up and make it more visible.

**Can I use anti-dandruff shampoo every day?**
Some are fine for frequent use, others aren't. Follow the label, and alternate with a gentle shampoo if your scalp gets dry.

**Does dandruff cause hair loss?**
Dandruff on its own isn't generally considered a cause of permanent hair loss, but heavy scratching and inflammation aren't good for the scalp. If you're losing hair, see a doctor.

**Can I get dandruff in my beard?**
Yes. The same yeast and oil process can cause flaking under a beard. Many of the same shampoo ingredients are used there, but check the label for facial use.

**Why is my scalp worse in winter?**
Cold air, low humidity, indoor heating and hot showers all dry out the skin, and many people find dandruff flares in colder months too.
