# The Modern Mullet: How to Get One That Actually Looks Good

The mullet came back, and it's stayed back longer than most people expected. It isn't the 1985 version, though. The modern mullet is shorter, more textured and much better blended, and when it's done well it looks like a choice rather than a joke.

Done badly, it still looks like the joke. The gap between the two comes down to a few specific decisions about the sides, the top and how much length you keep at the back, plus whether you have the patience to grow it properly.

Here's how to get a good one, and an honest look at who should skip it.

## Modern Mullet vs the 80s Mullet

The basic idea hasn't changed: shorter at the front and sides, longer at the back. Everything else has.

| | 80s mullet | Modern mullet |
|---|---|---|
| Sides | Fairly long, often feathered | Short: faded, tapered or tight scissor cut |
| Top | Flat or blow-dried with volume | Textured, often with a fringe or curls |
| Back | Long, heavy, one length | Shorter, layered, lighter at the ends |
| Transition | Abrupt shelf between top and back | Blended, connected through the crown |
| Overall effect | Two haircuts on one head | One cut with a clear shape |

The biggest difference is **connection**. A modern mullet flows from the top through the crown into the back, so the length at the nape looks like it belongs to the rest of the haircut. The 80s version often looked like the back had been forgotten.

## The Main Variations

**Burst fade mullet.** The most popular version right now. The sides fade in a curve around the ear, tight at the centre and blending out, and the back keeps its length. The short area around the ear makes the long back look deliberate.

**Taper mullet.** The sides are tapered (shortened gradually, still with some hair) rather than faded to skin. It's softer, grows out more gracefully and fits better in places where a skin fade looks too sharp.

**Scissor-cut mullet.** No clippers at all. The sides are cut short with scissors and blended into the top and back. It looks the most natural and the least obviously "done," and it works especially well on wavy hair.

**Curly mullet.** On curly or coily hair, the length at the back stacks up into a full, rounded shape instead of hanging down. With a burst fade it's one of the best-looking versions of the cut. ## Growing One Out: A Realistic Timeline

You can't get a mullet in one visit unless the back is already long. Most people start from a regular short haircut, and the grow-out is where most mullets fail.

Hair grows about half an inch a month, a bit faster or slower depending on the person. From a short back-and-sides:

- **Weeks 1–6:** Grow everything. Keep the sides tidy with a fade or taper every three to four weeks, but leave the back and top alone. This is the awkward stage, when it looks like a haircut that's overdue.
- **Weeks 6–10:** The back is now a couple of inches long, just enough to start shaping. A barber can clean up the neckline and the transition so it reads as a short mullet rather than overgrown hair.
- **Months 3–4:** The back has enough length for a proper modern mullet. Shape the back, connect it to the crown and decide how long you want it.
- **Month 4 onward:** Maintenance mode. Keep the sides tight and shape the back without taking off length unless you want to.

The key to getting through the awkward phase is **keeping the sides short the whole time**. A short, clean side area makes growing hair look deliberate, not neglected. Skip it and the whole head looks shaggy for two months.

## Who It Suits

**Good fit:**

- **Thick hair with some texture.** Wavy and curly hair are ideal, because the back has movement and doesn't hang flat.
- **Oval and longer faces**, which can carry the extra length at the back without looking out of proportion.
- **Strong jawlines**, which are framed well by short sides.
- **People who'll actually style it.** A mullet doesn't need much product, but it does need some attention.

**Weaker fit:**

- **Very fine, straight hair.** The back tends to hang in thin strands rather than forming a shape. It can work, but keep it shorter and more layered.
- **Thinning hair on top or a significantly receding hairline.** The mullet draws attention to the contrast between front and back, which isn't what you want if the front is sparse.
- **A thin or sparse nape.** If the hair at the back of your neck grows thinly, the long section won't look full.

If your hair type is one of those, our barbers will tell you before you spend three months growing it out. That's a better outcome than finding out at month three.

## The Workplace Question, Honestly

Some workplaces are fine with it. Plenty of offices, trades, creative jobs and hospitality roles won't notice. Others will: client-facing roles in finance, law and sales, and anywhere with a written grooming policy.

A few honest points:

- A **taper or scissor-cut mullet** with a modest back reads as "longer hair" to most people. A **burst skin fade mullet** with a long back reads as a mullet.
- You can wear the back tucked down or slightly pushed in for a meeting, but you can't hide it entirely.
- If you have a big interview coming up, think about timing. A mullet that's halfway grown out is the hardest version to wear in a formal setting.

## Maintenance and Styling

- **Sides:** every 2–4 weeks depending on the fade. A burst skin fade needs more frequent attention than a taper.
- **Back:** shaped every 6–8 weeks. Mostly removing weight and cleaning the neckline, not length.
- **Top:** trimmed with the sides so it stays connected to the back.

Styling is minimal. On wavy or curly hair, a light curl cream or sea salt spray on damp hair, then let it dry. On straight hair, a small amount of matte paste worked through the top and the back. Don't flatten the back with a heavy product; it should move.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long does my hair need to be at the back for a mullet?**
About two inches for a short modern mullet, and three to four inches for the fuller version. Curly hair usually needs a bit more length because it shrinks as it curls.

**Can I get a mullet if I have short hair now?**
Yes, but you'll need to grow the back for roughly three months. Keep the sides tidy in the meantime and it'll look intentional the whole way.

**Does a mullet need a fade?**
No. A taper or scissor-cut sides work just as well and look softer. The fade is a style choice.

**Is the mullet going out of style?**
Trends always shift, but the modern version has outlasted most predictions. If you stop liking it, it converts easily to a regular cut: the back just comes off.
