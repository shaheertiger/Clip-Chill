# Burst Fade Explained: What It Is and Who It Suits

Most fades run all the way around the head at one height. The burst fade doesn't. It fades in a curve around the ear, short at the centre and blending out in every direction, and then it stops. The back of the head keeps its length.

That one change is the reason it shows up under mohawks, frohawks, mullets and longer curly cuts. It gives you the clean, tight look of a fade at the sides while keeping weight and shape behind, which a full fade would remove.

It's also a cut people often ask for by the wrong name, or ask for without knowing what their hair will do with it. Here's how it works.

## What a Burst Fade Actually Is

Picture a semicircle centred on your ear. At the middle of that arc, right around and just above the ear, the hair is at its shortest: skin, a zero or a #1. Moving outward from the ear in any direction (up toward the temple, forward toward the sideburn, back toward the occipital bone), the hair gets gradually longer until it meets your full length.

The key points:

- **The fade is centred on the ear**, not on a horizontal line around the head.
- **The back is left long.** The nape and the area behind the ears keep their length, or they're cleaned up separately.
- **The top is usually longer and textured**, but that's up to you.

From the side, it looks like a short "sunburst" around the ear, which is where the name comes from.

## Burst Fade vs Drop Fade vs Regular Taper

These three get mixed up all the time, and asking for the wrong one gets you a noticeably different cut.

| | Burst fade | Drop fade | Regular taper |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shape of the fade line | Semicircle around the ear | Runs around the head, dips down behind the ear | Short only at sideburns and nape |
| Back of the head | Left long | Faded, following the curve | Mostly uniform length, cleaned at the neckline |
| Contrast | High at the ear, none at the back | High all the way around | Low |
| Best with | Mohawks, mullets, long curls | Short to medium tops, curls, waves | Conservative and longer cuts |

A **drop fade** still goes all the way around the head; it just curves down behind the ear instead of running straight across. The back is faded. A **burst fade** never reaches the back at all.

A **regular taper** only shortens the edges, the sideburns and the neckline, and leaves the sides fairly full. If you want minimal contrast, it's the right choice. The finish at the bottom, skin or taper, works the same way it does on any fade. Our breakdown of [skin fade vs taper fade](/skin-fade-vs-taper-fade) covers it in detail.

## What It Pairs With

**Mohawk and frohawk.** This is the classic pairing. Keeping length down the centre strip and the back, and fading only around the ears, gives a mohawk shape that looks wearable rather than costume-like. On coily hair, the burst frohawk is one of the cleanest versions of the style, because the dense texture holds a clear edge where the fade meets the length.

**Mullet.** The modern mullet depends on keeping length at the back, and a full fade would cut that off. A burst fade tightens the sides around the ear and leaves the back alone, so the length flows down without the sides looking heavy.

**Curly and wavy tops.** A burst fade under a full curly top takes away the bulk that makes curly hair look wide over the ears, and keeps enough at the back to stop the cut looking like a helmet. If curls are your situation, our [curly hair haircut guide](/curly-hair-mens-haircut-guide) is worth reading too.

**Textured crops** work with a burst fade too, though a mid or low fade is usually the more natural partner for a short top. With a crop, a burst can look a little unfinished at the back unless the back is tidied.

## Who It Suits

**Good fit:**

- Thick hair with enough density at the back to look full rather than straggly.
- Curly, coily and wavy textures, which hold a strong outline at the edge of the burst.
- Anyone growing out a mullet or mohawk who wants the sides tidy in the meantime.
- Rounder faces, because tightening the sides around the ears takes width away from the widest point.

**Not a great fit:**

- **Fine or thin hair at the back.** The point is the contrast between short sides and full length behind. If the back is sparse, that contrast doesn't happen and the cut just looks unfinished.
- **Very conservative workplaces.** A burst fade on its own is fairly low-key, but most of the styles it pairs with (mohawks, mullets) are not. Think about the whole cut, not just the sides.
- **Short hair all over.** If the back is already short, there's nothing for the burst to contrast against, and a regular low or mid fade will do the job better.

## How It Grows Out

The burst fade covers a small area, so it grows out more gracefully than a high fade. Regrowth shows around the ear first, usually within two weeks on a skin finish and three weeks on a taper finish.

The bigger maintenance question is usually the back. A mohawk or mullet that's left alone for six weeks stops looking like a deliberate style and starts looking like nobody's been cutting it. Planning for that is part of choosing the cut.

A realistic schedule:

- **Sides (the burst itself):** every 2–3 weeks to keep it crisp.
- **Back and top:** shaped every 4–6 weeks, more often if you're growing toward a specific length.

Tell your barber when you're growing the back. That way each visit tightens the burst and leaves the length alone.

## Styling at Home

Most burst fade styles need some product on top, because the cut relies on the top and back having shape.

- **Curly and coily hair:** a leave-in conditioner or curl cream applied while damp, then left alone to dry. Don't brush curls once they're dry.
- **Straight or wavy hair:** a matte clay or paste worked through dry hair, pushed up or forward depending on the style.
- **Mohawk strip:** a stronger-hold product or a bit of blow-drying upward to give it height.

Keep the faded area clean. Product built up around the ear shows easily against a skin finish.

## How to Ask for It

Be specific about the finish, the back and the top:

- "**Burst skin fade** around the ears, keep the back long, about three inches of curls on top."
- "**Burst taper**, start at a #1 around the ear, leave the back for a mullet, texture the top."
- "**Burst fade frohawk**, keep about two inches down the middle strip."

Bring a photo taken from the side. With a burst fade, how far the curve extends around the ear makes a real difference, and a picture shows that better than words.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is a burst fade the same as a drop fade?**
No. A drop fade runs all the way around the head and dips down behind the ear. A burst fade only curves around the ear and leaves the back long.

**Can I get a burst fade with short hair?**
You can, but the effect is weak if the back is also short. A low or mid fade usually looks better on short hair all over.

**Does a burst fade work on straight hair?**
Yes, especially with a mullet or a textured top. It looks most dramatic on curly and coily hair, but straight hair carries it fine as long as there's enough density at the back.

**How long does the hair at the back need to be?**
At least two to three inches to read as intentional. Less than that and it looks like a fade that wasn't finished.
