# Pomade vs Wax vs Clay vs Paste: Which Hair Product Do You Need?

Walk down any grooming aisle and you'll see dozens of tins that look the same and claim different things. Pomade, wax, clay, paste, putty, fibre, cream. Most men pick one at random, use too much of it, decide it doesn't work, and go back to whatever they had before.

The good news is that the four main products are easy to tell apart once you know what to look for. Each one is a trade-off between **hold** (how firmly hair stays put) and **shine** (how much it reflects light). Match those two to your hair type and haircut, and the choice makes itself.

## The Quick Comparison

| Product | Hold | Shine | Feel | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pomade | Medium to high | Medium to high | Smooth, slick | Side parts, slick backs, pompadours |
| Wax | Medium to high | Low to medium | Thick, tacky | Thick or coarse hair, piecey definition |
| Clay | High | Matte | Dry, gritty | Textured crops, fine hair, volume |
| Paste | Light to medium | Low to medium | Soft, pliable | Everyday styles, beginners, restyling |

Labels like "putty," "fibre," and "molding cream" are marketing names. They all sit somewhere on this chart. Check the hold and shine rating on the label, and ignore the rest.

## Pomade: Water-Based vs Oil-Based

Pomade is the classic product for polished, structured styles. It gives a clean, combed finish with visible shine. It comes in two very different types.

**Water-based pomade**
- Washes out easily with regular shampoo
- Can be restyled during the day with a bit of water
- Sets firmer as it dries
- The right choice for nearly everyone

**Oil-based pomade**
- Traditional, petroleum or wax-based
- Very long hold and high shine
- Stays workable all day without drying hard
- Hard to wash out and can build up over time
- Can contribute to clogged pores or breakouts along the forehead and hairline in some people

If you're not sure, buy water-based. Oil-based pomade suits men who want a vintage, wet-look finish and are prepared for the extra effort to wash it out.

## Wax

Wax is dense and tacky. It gives medium to strong hold with a natural or low-shine finish, and it's excellent for defining individual pieces of hair.

**Who it suits:** men with thick, coarse, or stubborn hair. Wax has the weight to control hair that ignores lighter products, and it's useful for flattening a cowlick that won't sit.

**Who should avoid it:** men with fine or thinning hair. The weight drags fine hair flat and makes it clump, which shows more scalp.

Wax can be hard to spread, so warming it properly before applying is essential.

## Clay

Clay is the go-to product for modern, textured cuts. It's made with natural clay, which absorbs oil and adds grip, so hair looks thicker and holds shape without looking styled.

**Who it suits:** almost anyone with short to medium straight or wavy hair, and particularly men with fine hair. It's the best product for making hair look fuller.

**Best cuts:** textured crops, messy quiffs, short spiky styles, French crops, and any cut over a fade where you want the top to look full and natural.

**Downsides:** it can feel stiff and slightly drying if used every day. It's also a poor fit for curly hair, which needs moisture rather than grip.

## Paste

Paste is the all-rounder. It has light to medium hold, a low to medium sheen, and stays flexible, so you can restyle with your fingers through the day.

**Who it suits:** beginners, men who want a natural finish, and anyone with medium-length hair that needs control without stiffness.

**Best cuts:** casual side parts, short to medium textured styles, and longer cuts that need light shaping.

If you only want to buy one product and you don't know where to start, buy a matte paste.

## Matching Product to Hair Type and Cut

**By hair type:**

- **Fine or thinning:** clay or a light paste. Avoid wax and oil-based pomade.
- **Thick and straight:** pomade or wax for structured styles, clay for textured ones.
- **Wavy:** paste or a light clay to define the wave without flattening it.
- **Curly or coily:** usually none of these. Curl creams and leave-in conditioners do a better job. See our [curly hair haircut guide](/curly-hair-mens-haircut-guide).

**By cut:**

- **Short textured crop over a fade:** clay
- **Classic side part or comb-over:** water-based pomade
- **Slick back or pompadour:** pomade (water or oil-based)
- **Messy medium-length style:** paste
- **Short spiky style:** wax or clay

Our [men's hair types guide](/mens-hair-types-guide) explains how to identify your type if you're not sure.

## How Much to Use

Much less than you think.

- **Short hair:** about the size of a pea
- **Medium hair:** about the size of a dime
- **Longer or very thick hair:** start with a dime and add if needed

Using too much is the most common reason a product "doesn't work." It makes hair greasy, heavy, and flat. You can always add a second small amount, but you can't take it out without washing.

## How to Apply It

1. **Start with the right hair.** Clay, paste, and wax work best on dry or nearly dry hair. Pomade can go on slightly damp hair for a smoother, wetter finish.
2. **Blow-dry first if you want volume.** Push the hair in the direction you want it to sit, and finish on the cool setting.
3. **Warm the product.** Rub it between your palms until it's thin, warm, and almost invisible. This step matters most with wax and clay.
4. **Apply back to front.** Start at the back and the roots, then work forward. Most men slap it on the front first and end up with a greasy fringe.
5. **Shape it.** Use your fingers for texture, or a comb for a clean finish with pomade.
6. **Finish with your fingertips.** Pinch and twist small sections on top for definition.

## Washing It Out

- **Water-based pomade, paste, and most clays:** come out with regular shampoo.
- **Wax and oil-based pomade:** harder. Apply shampoo to **dry** hair first, work it through, then add water and rinse. Repeat if needed.
- **Clarifying shampoo:** once a week if you use product daily, to remove buildup.
- **Conditioner afterward:** especially after clay, which can be drying.

Product left on the scalp for days can cause itchiness or flaking, so rinse properly.

## Common Mistakes

- Using too much
- Applying clay or wax to wet hair, which dilutes the hold
- Not warming the product before applying
- Putting it all on the front
- Using wax or oil-based pomade on fine hair
- Expecting the product to fix a cut that's grown out

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What's the difference between pomade and wax?**
Pomade is smoother and shinier, made for slick, structured looks. Wax is thicker, more matte, and better for defining pieces and controlling coarse hair.

**Is clay or paste better for fine hair?**
Clay. It adds grip and the appearance of thickness without weighing the hair down. A light paste is the next best option.

**Can I mix two products?**
Yes. Many men use sea salt spray before drying, then clay or paste after. Mixing a little paste with clay can soften the clay's stiffness too.

**Does hair product cause hair loss?**
No. Styling products sit on the hair and scalp surface and don't cause pattern hair loss. Wash properly to avoid buildup and irritation.
