# Blocked, Rounded or Tapered Neckline: Which Should You Ask For?

"How do you want the back?" It's one of the questions barbers ask most, and one of the ones clients are least sure how to answer. Most people shrug and say "whatever looks good," which is a fair answer, but it means you're leaving one of the most visible parts of your cut to chance.

The neckline is the part of your haircut other people see most. Every time you're standing in a queue, sitting in a meeting, or driving with someone in the back seat, the neckline is what they're looking at. It also decides how fast your cut looks grown out.

There are three main options. Here's what each one is, how it ages, and which suits you.

## The Three Necklines at a Glance

| Neckline | What it looks like | Grows out | Maintenance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blocked | Straight horizontal line across the nape | Fastest, regrowth visible in days | High |
| Rounded | Blocked line with softened corners | Fast, slightly softer than blocked | Medium to high |
| Tapered | Hair gets gradually shorter down to the natural hairline | Slowest, grows out naturally | Low |

## Blocked (Squared) Neckline

A blocked neckline is cut straight across the back of the neck, forming a clean horizontal edge, usually with squared corners at each side.

**What it does well:** it looks deliberate and very sharp the day it's cut. It also makes the neck look **wider**, which helps balance a long or slim neck.

**Where it falls down:** the moment hair starts to grow below the line, it shows. A blocked line has nothing to blend into, so regrowth under it looks untidy within a few days to a week. It's the highest maintenance neckline by a wide margin.

**Best with:** longer scissor cuts and classic styles where the hair at the back is full enough to support a hard line.

## Rounded Neckline

A rounded neckline is a blocked line with the corners curved off. It's a middle ground: still defined, but softer.

**What it does well:** it keeps a clean edge without the harsh corners, which suits rounder faces and softer styles. Some men find it looks less severe than a full block.

**Where it falls down:** it still shows regrowth relatively quickly because there's still a hard line at the bottom. The rounded corners can also look slightly dated on some modern cuts.

**Best with:** medium-length cuts and men who want a defined edge without a squared look.

## Tapered Neckline

A tapered neckline follows your natural hairline. Instead of a line, the hair gradually gets shorter as it goes down the neck until it disappears into the skin.

**What it does well:** it grows out naturally. Because there's no hard edge, new growth blends into the gradient, so it looks tidy for much longer. It also makes the neck look **longer and slimmer**.

**Where it falls down:** it's less dramatic than a blocked line. Some men prefer a sharper, more structured finish.

**Best with:** almost everything, and especially short cuts, fades, crops, and anyone who doesn't want to be in the chair every two weeks. If you have a fade, the neckline is usually tapered or faded already. Our [skin fade vs taper fade](/skin-fade-vs-taper-fade) guide covers how those finishes differ.

## How Each One Grows Out

This is often the deciding factor.

**Blocked:** within a week, stubble appears below the line. By two weeks, it looks like the cut is overdue, even if the rest of your hair is fine. Expect to need a tidy-up at least every two weeks to keep it looking intended.

**Rounded:** similar to blocked, though the softened corners make the regrowth very slightly less obvious.

**Tapered:** the regrowth fills in gradually, and it can look fine for three to four weeks or longer. It's the best option for men who get cuts less often.

If you're trying to stretch time between appointments, tapered is the clear choice.

## Which Suits Your Neck and Hairline?

**Thick or wide neck:** go tapered. A blocked line adds visual width, which isn't what you want. A taper draws the eye upward and slims the neck.

**Long or slim neck:** a blocked or rounded neckline adds width and balance. Tapered works too, but can make a long neck look longer.

**Low natural hairline:** if your hair grows far down your neck, a blocked line has to be cut well above where the hair naturally stops. That means a large shaved area that regrows fast and looks untidy quickly. A tapered neckline works with the hairline instead of against it.

**Cowlick or whorl at the nape:** hair that grows in different directions at the back of the neck is very common. A blocked line looks uneven as soon as that hair grows, because it grows sideways or upward. A short taper or fade at the nape is usually the best way to control it.

**Hairy back of the neck:** tapered, with the area below cleaned up. A blocked line will look messy quickly against dense growth below it.

## Should You Shave It Between Cuts?

You can, carefully. It can keep a blocked or rounded neckline looking sharp for an extra week. The risk is that every home touch-up tends to take the line a little higher, until your neckline sits far above where it should.

**How to do it without raising the line:**

1. **Only remove hair below the existing line.** Don't touch the line itself.
2. **Use two mirrors** so you can see what you're doing, or ask someone to help.
3. **Use a trimmer without a guard,** working downward and away from the line.
4. **Leave a small margin** above the stubble rather than cutting right up to the edge.
5. **Stop if unsure.** It's much easier to fix a few stray hairs than a line that's gone too high.

For a tapered neckline, don't try to recreate the taper at home. Just clean up obvious stray hair well below it.

If your neckline needs a fix, it's better to come in early than to try to correct it yourself.

## How to Ask Your Barber

Be specific. Any of these work:

- "Tapered at the back, following my natural hairline."
- "Blocked neckline, squared corners."
- "Rounded at the back."
- "Fade it out at the nape."

If you're not sure, ask your barber which one suits your neck and hairline. They can see the back of your head better than you can, and they'll be honest about what will grow out well. Bringing a photo of the back of a cut you like helps too. For more on describing what you want, see [how to ask for a haircut](/how-to-ask-for-a-haircut).

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Which neckline is the most popular?**
Tapered, by a long way. It suits most cuts, grows out well, and works with almost every neck shape.

**Can I switch from blocked to tapered?**
Yes, but it may take a cut or two. If the blocked line was cut high, the barber needs some regrowth below it to create a proper taper.

**Does a blocked neckline make my neck look thicker?**
It can. A horizontal line adds width, which is why blocked necklines suit slimmer necks better.

**How often should I get a blocked neckline tidied?**
Every one to two weeks if you want it to stay crisp. That's the main downside.

**Is a fade the same as a tapered neckline?**
Not exactly. A tapered neckline refers to the back of the neck. A fade usually takes the sides and back shorter in a gradient. Most fades include a faded or tapered neckline.
