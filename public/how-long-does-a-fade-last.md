# How Long Does a Fade Last? A Week-by-Week Breakdown

A fresh fade looks sharpest the day you walk out of the shop. The question every client eventually asks is how long it stays that way.

The honest answer is that it depends on the type of fade, how short it goes at the bottom, and how much you care about crisp edges. A skin fade can look noticeably grown out in under two weeks. A low taper can still look tidy after four.

Here's what actually happens week by week, and how to time your next appointment.

## How Fast Hair Grows

Scalp hair grows about **1.25 cm (half an inch) a month** on average. That works out to roughly **3 mm a week**.

Some men grow faster, some slower, and growth can vary with age, genetics, and season. But 3 mm a week is a useful rule of thumb, and it explains almost everything about how a fade grows out.

For reference, a #1 clipper guard leaves about 3 mm of hair. So in about a week, the shortest part of a skin fade has grown to roughly a #1.

## Why Skin Fades Show Growth Fastest

Every part of your head grows at roughly the same rate. What changes is how noticeable that growth is.

Adding 3 mm to hair that's 25 mm long on top is barely visible. Adding 3 mm to bare skin is a completely different look. The bottom of a skin fade goes from skin to stubble to a short, even layer, and the smooth transition from skin to hair, the thing that makes a skin fade a skin fade, is the first thing to disappear.

That's why:

- **Skin fades** (down to bare skin) lose their look fastest.
- **Taper fades** (shorter only at the sideburns and neckline) hold their shape longer.
- **Fades that stop at a #1 or #2** at the bottom grow out more gradually because the starting point already has length.

## Low, Mid and High Fades

The height of the fade matters as much as how short it goes.

**High fade.** Starts near the temples and the top of the head. There's a large area of very short hair, so regrowth shows over more of your head, and the blend softens quickly.

**Mid fade.** Starts around the middle of the sides, roughly level with the top of the ear. A balance between sharpness and maintenance.

**Low fade.** Starts just above the ears and neckline. There's less very short hair, so it grows out more gracefully and needs the least upkeep.

Combine the two variables and you get the full picture: a **high skin fade** is the highest maintenance fade there is. A **low taper** is the lowest.

## Week by Week: What a Fade Looks Like

**Week 1: Sharp**
For the first few days, the fade looks exactly as intended. By the end of the week, a skin fade has a visible shadow at the bottom, and the neckline and edges around the ears start to look a little soft. Most people won't notice. You will.

**Week 2: Softening**
The skin area of a skin fade has roughly 5 to 6 mm of growth, about the length of a #2. The transition between shades starts to blur. The lineup around the forehead and temples looks fuzzy. Taper fades and low fades still look tidy. For a skin fade, this is the point where many clients book.

**Week 3: Grown Out**
A skin fade now looks more like a short, even back and sides than a fade. Hair is growing around the ears and below the neckline. The top looks fine, but the overall shape has lost its contrast. Tapers and low fades are softening but still presentable.

**Week 4: Time for a Cut**
At around 12 mm of growth, almost every fade has lost its definition. The sides start to puff, especially on thick or wavy hair, and the shape of the cut starts to blur. Even a low taper is due for a refresh.

## Hair Type Makes a Difference

**Straight hair** shows every millimetre of regrowth, so fades on straight hair look grown out sooner.

**Thick or coarse hair** grows out bulkier and starts to stick out at the sides by week three.

**Curly and coily hair** hides some regrowth in the body of the cut, but the lineup and edges go fuzzy quickly, usually within a week or two.

**Light-coloured or grey hair** shows less contrast against the skin, so regrowth is less obvious.

## Lineup Touch-Ups

The edges, meaning the forehead line, temples, around the ears, and the neckline, are usually the first thing to look untidy. A clean edge can make a two-week-old fade look much fresher.

That said, once a fade is past the two-week mark, the blend has usually softened too, and cleaning up the edges alone won't bring it back. ## How to Make a Fade Last Longer

- **Choose a lower fade or a taper** if you can't come in every two weeks.
- **Ask for a fade that starts at a #1 or #0.5 rather than skin.** It grows out more evenly.
- **Keep the top a little longer.** More length on top keeps the overall shape balanced as the sides grow.
- **Book your next appointment before you leave.** It's the easiest way to stay on schedule.
- **Wash and condition regularly.** Healthy, clean hair sits better as it grows.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long does a skin fade last?**
About one to two weeks before it looks noticeably grown out. Most skin fade clients rebook every two to three weeks.

**Do taper fades last longer than skin fades?**
Yes. Because a taper only goes short at the sideburns and neckline, regrowth is far less obvious, and many clients go three to four weeks between cuts.

**Does hair grow faster after a haircut?**
No. Cutting hair doesn't change how fast it grows from the follicle. Short hair just makes growth more visible.

**Can I fix the fade myself between cuts?**
Not well. Blending a fade requires seeing the back and sides clearly and switching between guards precisely. Tidying the neckline is fine. Re-fading is usually best left to your barber.
