# Low, Mid or High Fade? How to Choose the Right Height

"Fade" tells your barber what kind of transition you want on the sides. It doesn't tell them where that transition sits, and the height changes the cut more than almost anything else you'll decide in the chair.

Put the same two inches on top, the same skin finish at the bottom, and move the fade line three centimetres up or down. You get three haircuts that look nothing alike. A low fade looks tidy and grown-up. A high fade looks sharp and deliberate, and you notice it from across the room.

Here's where each height actually starts, who it suits, and how each one behaves in week three, which is the part most people don't think about.

## Where Each Fade Height Starts

Barbers use your ear and temple as landmarks. The fade height is the point where the shortest length stops and the blend up to longer hair begins.

- **Low fade** starts just above the top of the ear in front and follows close to the natural hairline around the back. Most of the side of the head stays at your blending length, and only the bottom edge goes short.
- **Mid fade** starts roughly level with the top of the ear and the middle of the temple, then runs around the back at about the same height. Around half the side of the head is short.
- **High fade** starts above the temple, close to where the side of the head turns into the top (barbers call this the parietal ridge). Almost the whole side is short, and the length sits on top.

You can have any of these as a skin fade (down to bare skin) or a taper fade (shortest point still has some hair). Height and finish are separate choices. If the difference between those two finishes is new to you, start with our breakdown of [skin fade vs taper fade](/skin-fade-vs-taper-fade).

## The Comparison at a Glance

| | Low fade | Mid fade | High fade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starts at | Just above the ear | Top of the ear / mid-temple | Above the temple |
| Contrast | Subtle | Moderate | Strong |
| Looks best with | Medium to longer tops, side parts, curls | Almost anything | Short crops, flat tops, high-volume tops |
| Grows out | Most gracefully | Evenly | Fastest; shows within 1–2 weeks |
| Typical rebook | 3–4 weeks | 2–3 weeks | 2 weeks |
| Conservative workplace | Yes | Usually | Depends on the office |

## How Each Height Reads on Different Head Shapes

**Round faces and wider heads** usually look better with a mid or high fade. Taking the sides down tight removes width at the widest part of the head and pushes the eye upward, which makes the face look longer. A low fade on a round face can leave the sides looking full, especially if the top is also short.

**Long or narrow faces** are the opposite. A high fade adds height and takes away width, and on a face that's already long, it can make it look stretched. A low fade keeps some fullness at the sides and balances things out.

**Flat or uneven spots at the back of the head** are common, and most people don't know they have one until a high fade shows it. A low fade or a drop fade (which curves down behind the ear) leaves more hair over the crown and the occipital bone, the bump at the back of the skull, and hides a lot.

**Scars, birthmarks and bumps on the scalp** are worth mentioning before the clippers come out. A skin finish at mid or high height puts them on display. That's fine if you don't mind, and our barbers would rather know first.

Fade height is one of the easiest tools you have for adjusting proportions, and it costs nothing extra to get it right.

## How Hairline and Hair Type Change the Answer

**A receding hairline** tends to look better with a mid or high fade. When the sides are tight, the temples read as part of the shape of the cut rather than as missing hair. A low fade with fuller sides makes the recession easier to see. Our guide to the [best haircuts for a receding hairline](/best-haircuts-receding-hairline) goes further on this.

**Thick, coarse or coily hair** can carry any height, and a mid or high skin fade on tight curls gives some of the cleanest contrast you'll see. Remember that coily hair reads shorter than the guard number, so the blend zone has to be planned with that in mind.

**Fine or thinning hair** on top usually suits a low or mid fade. A high fade with a sparse top leaves very little hair anywhere, and the whole cut can start to look like a buzz that went wrong.

**Curly or wavy tops** with some length look natural over a low or mid fade. A high fade under a big curly top can look top-heavy unless that's the look you're going for.

## How Each Height Grows Out

The higher the fade, the more short hair you have, and the faster regrowth shows.

- **Low fade:** regrowth shows only along the bottom edge. At week three it still looks like a haircut, just a slightly softer one. This is the best height for anyone who stretches appointments.
- **Mid fade:** it holds well for about two weeks, then the blend starts to blur. At three weeks it's noticeable, and by four weeks it has turned into a loose taper.
- **High fade:** the large area of very short hair means half a centimetre of growth changes the whole look. Two weeks is realistic if you want it to stay sharp.

A skin finish at any height ages faster than a taper finish at the same height. If you want a high fade but can't get in every two weeks, a high taper (shortest point at a #1 or #0.5 instead of skin) is the practical compromise.

## Workplace Suitability, Honestly

Most workplaces in Mississauga don't care about fade height. Some still do: client-facing finance and law, some hospitality roles, uniformed services, and anywhere with a written grooming standard.

- **Low fade:** fits anywhere. Nobody has ever been sent home for one.
- **Mid fade:** fits nearly anywhere, especially as a taper, or as a skin fade with a conventional top.
- **High skin fade:** reads as a style choice. Paired with a neat top, it's fine in most offices. Paired with a hard part, a design or a dramatic top, it stands out more than some employers would like.

If you've got an interview coming up and you're not sure, go low or mid. You can always go higher next time. More on this in our [job interview haircut guide](/haircut-for-job-interview).

## How to Ask for It

Be specific about three things: the height, the finish at the bottom, and what's on top.

- "**Low skin fade**, two inches on top, scissor cut, natural neckline."
- "**Mid taper**, starting at a #1, blended up to a #4, leave the top long enough to push back."
- "**High skin fade**, short textured crop on top."

If you're unsure, point. Put a finger where you want the shortest part to stop, and our barbers will work from that. A reference photo that clearly shows the side of the head helps even more.

You can also start lower. A barber can always take a fade higher in the same sitting, but bringing it down means waiting for it to grow.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Which fade height is most popular?**
The mid fade, by a wide margin. It has enough contrast to look current and enough hair left on the sides to suit most head shapes and most workplaces.

**Is a low fade the same as a taper?**
No. A low fade sets the height and a taper sets the finish. You can have a low taper (low height, some hair left at the bottom) or a low skin fade (low height, down to skin). People do mix the terms up, which is why it helps to name both.

**Can I switch heights between appointments?**
Going higher is easy, even in one visit. Going lower takes about two to four weeks of growth before there's enough hair to rebuild the fade lower down.

**Does a high fade make my head look bigger?**
It can make the top look more dominant, because the sides are so short. On a smaller head or a longer face, that can look out of proportion. A barber will tell you if they think it'll happen.
